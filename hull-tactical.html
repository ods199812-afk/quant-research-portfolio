01 / Research question

How much structure is there in noisy market targets?

The research began with a practical modeling question: can the available historical inputs support a prediction of the dataset's forward_returns target, or do apparent relationships fail to persist out of sample? A secondary line of inquiry looked at volatility and rolling risk characteristics as potentially more structured targets than short-horizon signed returns.

The goal was to understand the information content and limitations of the dataset rather than assume that adding model complexity would generate a tradable edge.

03 / Work completed

Experiments, not just model names

Baseline
Ridge regression

Investigated a regularized linear mapping and used its behavior to frame whether apparent predictability required a complex model.

Nonlinear comparison
LightGBM

Explored relationships beyond a linear mapping, with attention to the difference between in-sample fit and time-ordered out-of-sample behavior.

Feature assessment
Anonymous features, PCA and diagnostic plots

Reviewed V-feature behavior, transformations and feature importance / stability, recognizing that anonymization limits economic interpretation.

Alternative target
Rolling variance and volatility

Tested lagged-variance predictors and investigated variance-forecast evaluation, including QLIKE, as a complementary research direction.

04 / What the exploratory work showed

Lessons from weak signals and unstable models

Signal strength

Low signal-to-noise

Simple feature correlations and model fit did not provide convincing evidence of a robust return-forecasting signal in the experiments reviewed.

Interpretability

Anonymous features

Opaque variables complicated hypothesis formation and made it difficult to link a statistical pattern to an economic mechanism.

Model discipline

Evaluation matters

The work shifted emphasis toward time-aware validation, reasonable baselines and understanding why model improvements fail to generalize.

Reporting boundary. This page documents exploratory work and methodological lessons. It does not report a competition rank, winning model, final score, trading strategy, live Sharpe ratio, or independently verified out-of-sample investment performance. Numerical experiment outputs and reproducible notebooks have not been included in this public case study.
05 / Relevance

Research judgment for systematic strategies

Many financial modeling tasks are constrained less by the choice between Ridge and boosting than by target definition, data availability, changing distributions and the translation from statistical forecasts to decisions. This exploratory project emphasizes those research constraints and complements the more complete regime-conditioned factor case study and portfolio optimization toolkit.
